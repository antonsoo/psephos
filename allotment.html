    The procedure and the experiment.

    What the text supplies

    Two devices per tribe (63.2); ten approximately equal letter groups within each tribe (63.4); a chosen ticket inserter and five frames per device (64.2); white and black bronze cubes, one per five tickets, with the inserter also counted (64.3); a further allotment to a court (64.4).

    What you are operating

    One schematic five-column board for ordinary tickets in five letter groups of one tribe. A–E and the numbers are modern identifiers, not copies of Greek inscriptions. Four, six or eight full rows; no uneven attendance or empty slots. The inserter is outside these tickets. The row chances do not compute a historical jury quota. Drawing every row is an explanatory completion, not a claim about historical stopping rules.

    What the numbers assume

    Fixed placement; an unbiased order of a fixed supply of white and black cubes. The chance calculation is exact under these assumptions. Moving and shuffling tickets are experiments in how placement matters, not evidence that a historical official could arrange it freely. No result establishes how tamper-resistant the real process was.

    Who is absent

    This was not a lottery of all Athenians. Jury service concerned eligible adult male citizens, with age and status restrictions (63.3); women, enslaved people and resident foreigners were excluded. The service pool is not a single court, and selection says nothing about a later verdict.
